On dreaming

Integration is key.The real work begins after the session, after the retreat, after the medicine ceremony.

In my Yoga Nidra sessions, too, the integration circles are very close to my heart. Creating space and holding it. For what was experienced. What was felt. Sharing with one another. Answering questions and letting answers arise from within ourselves and between us. Or receiving the silence we hold together as an answer.

In the circles after the Nidra journeys, the topic of DREAMS naturally comes up often. And so over all these years I have been told an incredible number of dreams. It feels like thousands (I really have guided countless Yoga Nidra journeys & like to mention that it is one of my most used medicines!).

I could write a book about it (one day perhaps, if someone gives me a time-turner, hello Hermione! 🙋🏽‍♀️).

The great variety, the many facets and the complexity of the meaning of dreams have always deeply impressed me. Slowly but surely, through my depth-psychological and Jungian approaches, my work with the subconscious, archetypes and hypnosis, and through my clairsentience, I began to recognise patterns.

Yet I am very careful with classic dream interpretation. I would never presume to simply ~ interpret ~ another soul's dream.

Many paths lead through symbols, metaphors and myths into dream analysis. There are many different forms of this art. They differ greatly in cultural history and socio-culturally. And of course on a personal, individual level, from soul to soul.

An interpretation can only happen in deep and sensitive exchange with that soul itself. Together. With the power of the Inner Child, the subconscious, the unconscious, the ancestors and the divine source.
